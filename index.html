Our Philosophy

We back the candidates most likely to actually improve the most lives
without trampling anyone else's in the process.

Progress is not a purity test. It is a ledger: lives improved, rights protected, problems solved, weighed against what it cost to get there and who paid that cost. We watch that ledger.

A candidate who cannot build a coalition cannot build a bike lane. Passion is the starting point, not the credential. We back whoever gives us the best shot at measurable, durable progress, even when that's not our first fantasy pick.

"The best is not the one who is always right, but the one who gets things right often enough to matter, and builds enough trust to do it again."

Not a crusade. Not a brand. A working theory about how change actually happens in a large, complicated city where everyone has something at stake. We are progressives who believe in winning elections and then governing well. Both halves matter.

Statewide Proposition

Proposition 39 · Voter ID

Our Position
Vote No

Our Rationale

Prop 39 would amend the state constitution to require government-issued ID every time a Californian votes, add an ID number to every mail ballot envelope, and order counties to re-verify the citizenship of people already on the rolls. The Legislative Analyst puts the cost in the tens to low hundreds of millions of dollars a year. That is real money spent to make voting harder for seniors, renters who move, and working people without a current ID, in exchange for solving a problem California's elections do not have. Apply the ledger: lives improved, none; rights protected, fewer. No.

Statewide Proposition

Proposition 4 · Public Campaign Financing

Our Position
Vote Yes

Our Rationale

Prop 4 repeals the statewide ban on public financing of campaigns. It does not create a program; it lets cities, counties and the state build one if their voters want it, with guardrails on where the money comes from and who qualifies. Los Angeles already runs matching funds and it is one of the few reasons a candidate without a donor network can compete here. Letting the rest of California make that choice is cheap, practical, and overdue. Yes.
